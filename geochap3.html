Chapter 3: Physiography and Drainage

What is Physiography?

Physiography refers to the study of the physical features of the Earth's surface. It deals with landforms, their origin, and the processes that shape them. The Earth’s surface consists of mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys, and other features that are formed due to various geological processes.

Physiographic Divisions of India

India’s physiography can be divided into the following divisions:

  • Himalayan Mountains: These are the youngest and highest mountain ranges, which run along the northern boundary of India.
  • Indo-Gangetic Plain: A large fertile plain formed by the Ganges and its tributaries, covering much of northern India.
  • Peninsular Plateau: A large region of old, stable landforms, including hills and valleys, located in the southern part of India.
  • Coastal Plains: Narrow strips of land along the east and west coasts of India.
  • Islands: India also has many islands, including the Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal and the Lakshadweep Islands in the Arabian Sea.

What is Drainage?

Drainage refers to the system by which water is discharged from the surface of the Earth. It involves the study of rivers, lakes, and other water bodies that flow across the land. In terms of drainage, India has several major river systems.

Major Drainage Systems of India

India’s drainage systems are formed by a number of rivers and their tributaries. The two major types of drainage systems in India are:

  • Peninsular Drainage: Rivers like the Godavari, Mahanadi, and Narmada flow towards the east or west and drain into the Bay of Bengal or the Arabian Sea.
  • Himalayan Drainage: Rivers like the Ganges, Indus, and Brahmaputra, which are primarily fed by melting snow from the Himalayas and flow towards the east or west.
